DEAF CULTURE 101

Start here. Learn to see the world the way Deaf readers already do.

A friendly primer for hearing people, parents of Deaf children, and anyone new to the community. Get the foundations, learn the social norms, meet the history — and unlearn the myths that keep getting in the way.

01 · ETIQUETTE & SOCIAL NORMS

How to interact with respect

Most awkward moments come from good intentions and missing context. These are the norms Deaf readers tell us they wish more hearing people knew — save them, share them.

Do
  • Wave, tap a shoulder, or flick the lights to get attention.
  • Face the person, keep your hands and mouth visible, speak at a normal pace.
Don't
  • Don't shout, exaggerate mouth shapes, or over-enunciate.
  • Don't say 'never mind' when asked to repeat. Rephrase and try again.

02 · HISTORY & HERITAGE

A community with a long memory

Deaf history is a story of language reclaimed — of schools, protests, artists and educators who kept sign alive through eras that tried to erase it.

1760
First public Deaf school

Abbé de l'Épée opens the Paris school that seeds modern sign-language education across Europe and, later, the Americas.

1817
American School for the Deaf

Thomas Gallaudet and Laurent Clerc found the first permanent U.S. school for the Deaf — the birthplace of American Sign Language.

1880
The Milan Conference

Hearing educators vote to ban sign language in schools. Generations of Deaf children are denied their natural language — a wound the community still names today.

1988
Deaf President Now

Gallaudet University students shut down campus until a Deaf president is appointed. A defining moment of Deaf civil-rights organizing.

TODAY
Deaf-led culture

Deaf filmmakers, poets, athletes and educators are shaping mainstream stages — from Oscar wins to Broadway to the Deaflympics.

03 · MYTH-BUSTING LIBRARY

Myths, gently corrected

You don't have to be embarrassed if you believed any of these — most hearing people do. What matters is what you do with the correction.

MYTH 01"Sign language is universal."
FACTThere are 300+ distinct sign languages. ASL and BSL share almost nothing — they aren't dialects of each other.
MYTH 02"All Deaf people can read lips."
FACTEven skilled lip-readers catch only 30-45% of English on the lips. Lipreading is a supplement, not a substitute for access.
