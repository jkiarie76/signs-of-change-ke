Communication Etiquette

A practical guide to respectful interaction, clear engagement boundaries, and inclusive conversation dynamics.

Do

Tap the Shoulder Softly

Gently tap the person once or twice on the shoulder or upper arm. Alternatively, if you are at a distance, emit a small, polite wave within their direct line of sight to signal your presence naturally.

Don't

Avoid Aggressive Gestures

Never poke, touch the face or head, or grab someone's hands to stop them from signing. Avoid throwing objects or waving your hands frantically inches away from their face, as this is highly disruptive.

Do

Maintain Direct Eye Contact

Look directly at the person you are communicating with. Keep your mouth fully visible while speaking, use clear facial grammar, and maintain a standard conversational speaking volume and pace.

Don't

Don't Exaggerate or Cover Mouth

Never shout or unnaturally over-enunciate words, as this distorts lip shapes and makes visual context tracking harder. Do not block your mouth with your hands, papers, or look away mid-sentence.

Do

Address the Individual directly

Always maintain eye contact with the deaf individual and speak using direct first-person phrasing (e.g., "What is your preference?"). The interpreter will convey everything accurately in parallel.

Don't

Don't Look at the Interpreter

Avoid facing the sign language interpreter or speaking to them in the third person (e.g., "Tell him to sign this form"). This excludes the deaf person from the immediate conversational circle.
