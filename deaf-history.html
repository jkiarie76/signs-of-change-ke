Historical Milestones

An interactive chronicle tracking the global evolution of visual languages and foundational civil rights milestones across Kenya.

1755
Global Foundation

The Father of Public Deaf Education

Abbé Charles-Michel de l'Épée establishes the world's first free public school for the deaf in Paris, France. He blends natural community signs into a formalized manual language structure, proving to the world that visual language handles complex abstract concepts fluently.

1864
Academic Milestone

Inauguration of Gallaudet University

The world’s first higher-education institution specifically tailored for deaf individuals opens its doors in Washington, D.C.. This sets a permanent global standard for high-level scholastic achievement and deaf leadership pipelines.

1958
Kenyan Landmark

First Specialized Deaf Units in Kenya

Documented structures for specialized deaf education pop up across Kenya. Regional school networks begin forming, igniting the natural convergence and evolution of what would formally become Kenyan Sign Language (KSL).

1987
Institutionalization

Formal Registration of KNAD

The Kenya National Association of the Deaf (KNAD) registers formally under the Registrar of Societies Act. This establishes a unified national governing body to systematically advocate for the constitutional and linguistic rights of deaf individuals nationwide.

2010
Constitutional Victory

Official Recognition of KSL

A historic stride for inclusion: the promulgation of the new Constitution of Kenya officially recognizes Kenyan Sign Language (KSL) as a national language and an official means of parliamentary processing.
